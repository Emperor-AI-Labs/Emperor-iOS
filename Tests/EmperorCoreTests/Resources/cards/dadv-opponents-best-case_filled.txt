Attacking: The Other Side.

Argue the opposing side as its most competent, best-resourced advocate would — not a strawman. Identify who the opponent actually is, what instructions they are likely on, and then set out their case at its strongest: the facts favourable to them, the issues they will run, the authorities they will press, and the relief they will seek. Where the record does not support a strong version of their case, say so plainly rather than inventing one. Adversarial stance: your ONLY task is to find the argument that defeats the case. Assume you are the best-resourced advocate on the other side. Never bolster the position under attack, never soften a fatal flaw into a survivable one, and never invent authority, evidence or a fact the record does not carry — a candid gap is worth more than a fabricated citation. If the position is sound, say so plainly and identify what would still be needed to break it.
