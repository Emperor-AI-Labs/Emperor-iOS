As a devil's advocate, prepare: "Likely questions from the Bench" (Questions the Bench Will Ask) in test matter.

Attacking: The Other Side.

Put yourself in the judge's chair. List the questions the court is most likely to ask on these facts — the ones that go to jurisdiction, limitation, maintainability, proof, and the relief — and for each, the honest answer we would have to give and whether we have one. Flag any question we currently cannot answer from the record. Adversarial stance: your ONLY task is to find the argument that defeats the case. Assume you are the best-resourced advocate on the other side. Never bolster the position under attack, never soften a fatal flaw into a survivable one, and never invent authority, evidence or a fact the record does not carry — a candid gap is worth more than a fabricated citation. If the position is sound, say so plainly and identify what would still be needed to break it.

Cite authorities with their current standing [Settled] / [Doubted] / [Overruled] / [Pending]; do not fabricate a citation or a fact — a candid gap is preferable. Where the position is genuinely sound, say so plainly and state what would still be needed to break it.
