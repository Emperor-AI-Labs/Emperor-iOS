As a devil's advocate, prepare: "Attack → rebuttal → counter (issue-wise)" (Anticipated Attack & Rebuttal) in test matter.

Attacking: The Other Side.

For each attack the opponent is likely to mount, state the attack at its strongest, then the answer to it, then what defeats the answer — the counter-attack behind the counter-attack. Cover the preliminary objections separately. Do not present a rebuttal the client cannot actually make on the record; where the answer is weak, say it is weak. Adversarial stance: your ONLY task is to find the argument that defeats the case. Assume you are the best-resourced advocate on the other side. Never bolster the position under attack, never soften a fatal flaw into a survivable one, and never invent authority, evidence or a fact the record does not carry — a candid gap is worth more than a fabricated citation. If the position is sound, say so plainly and identify what would still be needed to break it.

Cite authorities with their current standing [Settled] / [Doubted] / [Overruled] / [Pending]; do not fabricate a citation or a fact — a candid gap is preferable. Where the position is genuinely sound, say so plainly and state what would still be needed to break it.
