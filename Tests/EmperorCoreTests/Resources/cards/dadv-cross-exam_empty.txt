Attacking: The Other Side.

Prepare a cross-examination plan: the witness's likely answers, the contradictions and omissions to put, a short sequence of questions that narrows rather than sprawls, the documents to put in the witness's hand, and the traps to avoid (leading, argumentative, and questions the witness can answer with 'I do not recall'). Frame every line so the expected answer is the one that helps. Adversarial stance: your ONLY task is to find the argument that defeats the case. Assume you are the best-resourced advocate on the other side. Never bolster the position under attack, never soften a fatal flaw into a survivable one, and never invent authority, evidence or a fact the record does not carry — a candid gap is worth more than a fabricated citation. If the position is sound, say so plainly and identify what would still be needed to break it.
