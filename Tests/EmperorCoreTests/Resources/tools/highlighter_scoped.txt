Run a NUMBERS-AND-TERMS EXTRACTION over the material supplied. This is extraction, NOT a summary — the reader already knows broadly what the document says; what they cannot do is read 200 pages to find the four figures that decide the matter. Your whole value is that every material number, period, cap, rate and hard term is captured EXACTLY as written and pinned to the place it appears. Do not write a narrative summary of the document at any point.

The unit is the INSTRUMENT — the agreement, deed, notice, award or order in front of you, read together with its own schedules, annexures, amendments and side letters. An inconsistency here means an inconsistency INSIDE that family of papers; reading a set of separate documents against each other is a different tool (Doc Index), so do not turn this into a cross-document reconciliation.

EXTRACT: Caps, Indemnity Limits & Carve-outs — this run is narrowed to that category. Go exhaustively through it, and still produce the SCANNED, AT A GLANCE, DISCREPANCIES and NOT IN THE RECORD sections, which apply always.
READING FOR: the Buyer. For every cap, penalty, interest charge, indemnity, deposit and notice obligation, mark whether it runs AGAINST this party (exposure) or IN ITS FAVOUR (entitlement); where it cuts both ways, say so.
HUNT SPECIFICALLY FOR: the liability cap. If it is in the material, it is the FIRST ROW of AT A GLANCE, and it also gets its own full row, in full detail, in whichever category table it belongs to. If it genuinely is not there, say so in terms in NOT IN THE RECORD rather than offering the nearest thing.

TEXT SUPPLIED BY THE USER:
Clause 9.2 caps liability at INR 5,00,00,000.

FOUR RULES THAT GOVERN EVERY LINE YOU WRITE

1. REPRODUCE FIGURES EXACTLY. Copy each amount character-for-character as the document writes it — the Rs. / INR / ₹ marker, the digit grouping, the "/-", and the words-in-brackets form in full: "Rs. 1,50,00,000/- (Rupees One Crore Fifty Lakh only)", never "Rs. 15 million", "1.5 cr" or a re-grouped "Rs. 150,00,000". Indian grouping takes the last three digits together and then runs in twos — 1,50,00,000 reads 1 | 50 | 00 | 000, one crore fifty lakh. Read with Western three-digit grouping the same digit string comes out as 1,500,000, fifteen lakh, a tenth of the real figure, and that is the misread to guard against: count the commas from the right deliberately before you rely on any eight- or nine-digit amount. Never convert, round, normalise or abbreviate, and never drop the words in brackets. Carry the unit and the period with every figure — per annum, per month, per sq. ft., per incident, in the aggregate — because a rate without its period is a wrong number.
   - FOREIGN CURRENCY: reproduce a USD, EUR, GBP, AED or other foreign amount exactly as written, with its currency marker. Where the same obligation is expressed in both a foreign currency and rupees, capture BOTH figures, the conversion mechanic (the rate, its source — RBI reference rate, SBI TT selling rate, an agreed fixed rate — and the reference date on which it is struck), and where a cap or price is "the lower of" or "the higher of" the two, quote that formula verbatim and say which limb governs if the document itself fixes it. That formula is frequently the number the whole matter turns on.
   - NON-ROMAN SCRIPT: an amount written in Devanagari or a State language — common on stamp endorsements, receipts and older deeds — is reproduced as it appears, with the transliteration beside it marked "(transliterated)".
   - "(COMPUTED)": any figure you derive rather than read is marked "(computed)" immediately after it, with the inputs and the clause the formula comes from — an outside date worked out from a start point and a period, a cap expressed as a multiple of fees or as fees paid in the preceding twelve months, a percentage applied to the consideration, a total of instalments. An unmarked figure in this extract carries one meaning only: those characters appear on the page cited. Never let a derived number pass as a quoted one.
   - If a digit is illegible or the page is a poor scan, give your best reading followed by [figure unclear in source]; never a clean guess.

2. PIN-CITE EVERY VALUE. No number appears anywhere without its source beside it, in the form: document file name, page, and clause / paragraph / schedule / annexure / exhibit — "Facility Agreement.pdf, p. 27, cl. 8.3(b)" or "Lease Deed.pdf, p. 4, Sch. II". Say when the value sits in a schedule, annexure, definitions clause, recital, rate table or side letter rather than the operative clause, because that is usually where the real number hides. If you cannot establish the page, cite the clause and write "page not determinable" — do NOT invent a page number, a clause number or a document name. Where the meaning turns on the wording, quote the operative words verbatim beside the figure ("shall not exceed, in the aggregate" is a different animal from "shall not exceed, per claim").

3. SWEEP THE WHOLE INSTRUMENT. Do not extract only from the passage in front of you; work front to back through every page, including the definitions clause, all schedules, annexures and appendices, the payment and rate tables, the signature page, the stamp endorsement, and any addendum, amendment or side letter. On the pages you read, hunt the markers: every currency symbol, every percentage, every occurrence of lakh or crore, every count of days / months / years, and every phrase of the "shall not exceed / limited to / capped at / whichever is higher / whichever is earlier / within ___ days of" family. Where an amendment restates a figure, extract both the original and the amended figure and state which prevails. Where the record is too long to read end to end, follow the block below rather than quietly sampling it.

4. ABSENCE IS A FINDING. Where an expected term simply is not there, record that. Do not supply a market-standard figure and do not put a [placeholder] in a value column — a placeholder there is indistinguishable from a fabricated number. Write "Not stated in the record" and, for the material ones, add the consequence in one line (no liability cap means uncapped exposure; no clause on interest for delayed payment throws the claim back on statute; an arbitration clause naming no seat is a live problem).

WHERE THE RECORD IS LONGER THAN YOU CAN READ IN FULL
This tool exists for the long record, so a partial sweep is the normal case, not the failure case. The failure is a partial sweep presented as a complete one. Therefore:
- Call get_document_map on each attached file FIRST and work from its layout rather than reading from page 1 until you run out of room.
- Read, in this priority: the definitions clause; the consideration, payment and interest clauses; every schedule, annexure and rate table (a rate table is read whole or not at all); limitation of liability and indemnity; security and guarantee; termination and default; tax, stamp and registration including the endorsement; the dispute-resolution clause; the signature page; and every amendment, addendum and side letter.
- To locate what the map does not label, use search_knowledge_base — it is a MEANING-based search, so ask it in plain prose ("the limit on liability and the exceptions to that limit", "the payment schedule and the amount payable at each milestone", "interest payable on delayed payment"). Do not fire bare markers like "Rs." or "%" at it; those are what you look for with your eyes once the pages are in front of you. Then read_raw_pages over the pages it returns, plus a page either side, because a figure severed from its proviso is a wrong figure.
- Then say what you did not read. In SCANNED, give the page ranges actually read AND the page ranges NOT read, by number ("pp. 412-1,860 not read"). For an extraction tool a stated gap costs the reader nothing and a silent one costs him the case.

OUTPUT STRUCTURE — a heading, then tables, in this order

SCANNED: one line per document — its file name, the page ranges you actually read, and the page ranges you did not read (or "read in full" where you read all of it). Name any part you could not read because it is illegible, missing or truncated. Do not describe a page you have not read.

AT A GLANCE: a short table of the five to eight figures that actually decide this matter, most material first — Term | Value as written | Where. Nothing else in this table.

Then produce EXACTLY ONE category table, under the heading <h2>CAPS, LIMITS & CARVE-OUTS</h2>, drawn from the category checklist below. Go through that category exhaustively: every value of that kind in the instrument, not a representative sample, in a table with the columns: Item | Value exactly as written | Trigger or condition | Where (document, page, clause) | Effect on the Buyer. Do NOT produce the other category tables. Immediately after that table, in one line, name the categories you have not produced on this run so the reader knows exactly what is missing and can re-run with "Key Numbers & Critical Terms (everything material)" to get them.

- CONSIDERATION & PAYMENT: price, fee, rent, salary or contract value; the payment schedule milestone by milestone with each due date; advance and mobilisation amounts; invoicing and credit period; escalation with its index, percentage and frequency; minimum guarantee, take-or-pay and price-revision mechanics.
- INTEREST, PENALTY & LIQUIDATED DAMAGES: the rate for delayed payment, whether simple or compounded and at what rests, penal or default interest, LD rate per day or week and its ceiling, forfeiture, and every "whichever is higher" formula written out. Where the payee is a registered micro or small enterprise supplier, add one line that the contractual rate may not be the rate that governs: ss. 15-16 of the MSMED Act, 2006 fix an outer limit of 45 days for payment and compound interest with monthly rests at three times the bank rate notified by the RBI, and that overrides the agreement.
- CAPS, LIMITS & CARVE-OUTS: the aggregate liability cap and how it is measured (a fixed sum, a multiple of fees, fees paid in the preceding twelve months); per-claim sub-caps; de minimis threshold and basket, and whether the basket tips or applies only to the excess; indemnity limits and their survival period; the EXPRESS CARVE-OUTS FROM THE CAP — fraud, wilful misconduct, breach of confidentiality, IP infringement, data protection, third-party indemnity, payment obligations — listed individually even though they carry no figure, because they are the heads of unlimited liability; and the exclusion of indirect or consequential loss, noting specifically whether loss of profit is excluded.
- SECURITY & CREDIT SUPPORT: security deposit and its refund mechanics, EMD or bid security, performance bank guarantee with amount, validity and claim period, retention money and its release milestones, advance BG, corporate or personal guarantee and the amount guaranteed, and any mortgage, hypothecation or pledge with the secured amount.
- TIME, NOTICE & DEADLINES: term and commencement date, lock-in, renewal or extension window, the termination notice period for EACH party separately (they are frequently different, and that difference matters), exclusivity or standstill, the claim-notification and survival windows, force-majeure notice, and time-is-of-the-essence wording. Where a clause fixes both a start point and a period, give the computed outside date, marked "(computed)" under Rule 1. Where a clause shortens the limitation period, or extinguishes a right or discharges a party once a contractual window expires, flag it against s. 28 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 — an internationally drafted agreement carries such a clause as a matter of course and in India it is void, subject to the exception for a guarantee in favour of a bank or financial institution where the specified period is not less than one year.
- TERMINATION, DEFAULT & CONDITIONS PRECEDENT: every termination trigger, one row each — termination for convenience, material breach, payment default, insolvency or the admission of a s. 7 / s. 9 application under the IBC, 2016, change of control, cross-default with its threshold figure, a material adverse change and how MAC is defined, and any regulatory or licence trigger. For each: which party may terminate on it, the notice required, the cure period and whether the cure period runs from the breach or from the notice, and the consequence on termination (refund, forfeiture, exit payment, transition obligation). Then the conditions precedent, listed individually with who bears each and its deadline, the long-stop date and what happens if it passes, and any condition subsequent.
- TAX, STAMP & REGISTRATION: GST rate, who bears it and whether the price is inclusive or exclusive, reverse charge, TDS rate with the section and any gross-up obligation, the stamp duty recited against the stamp actually affixed and the State whose stamp law applies (flag any shortfall or missing endorsement — an insufficiently stamped instrument is liable to be impounded and is inadmissible until duty and penalty are paid, under s. 35 of the Indian Stamp Act, 1899 or the corresponding provision of the applicable State Stamp Act, such as ss. 33-34 of the Maharashtra Stamp Act, 1958; cite the provision of the State whose law governs, not the Central Act by default), and registration where the instrument requires it (s. 17 of the Registration Act, 1908, a lease for a term exceeding one year being the common trap — and give the consequence under s. 49: the unregistered instrument does not affect the immovable property and is not receivable as evidence of the transaction, save for a collateral purpose).
- FORUM, LAW & DISPUTE CLAUSE: governing law; seat of arbitration; venue; institution and rules; number of arbitrators and the appointment mechanism; language; the jurisdiction clause and whether it is exclusive; pre-arbitration steps with their time limits; and any monetary threshold that routes a dispute to a different track. Flag squarely where seat and venue are named inconsistently, or where only a "venue" is named and no seat, since the seat fixes the court with supervisory jurisdiction.
- DEFINED TERMS WITH TEETH: every defined term whose definition itself carries a number, a period, a formula or a condition — the operative clause reads harmlessly until the definition is read against it. Give the definition verbatim and the clause it bites in.

DISCREPANCIES & INTERNAL INCONSISTENCIES: this is often the single most valuable thing this extract produces, so run it deliberately rather than in passing. Compare each figure against every other appearance of the same figure and report: (a) numerals that do not match the amount written in words in the same clause — "Rs. 1,50,00,000/- (Rupees Fifteen Lakh only)" — reported even where the intended figure seems obvious; on a negotiable instrument (cheque, promissory note, bill of exchange) s. 18 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 settles it in favour of the amount in words, but in an ordinary commercial contract s. 18 has no application, so give both versions and flag the conflict for construction rather than declaring a winner; (b) the same amount, rate, period or date stated differently in two places (operative clause against schedule, recital, annexure or rate table); (c) arithmetic that does not close — instalments that do not total the consideration, percentages that do not reach 100, schedule line items that exceed the cap; (d) cross-references to a clause, schedule or annexure that is missing, misnumbered, or does not say what the reference claims; (e) periods that cannot both be satisfied, such as a cure period longer than the notice that triggers it. Table it as: What conflicts | Version A with pin-cite | Version B with pin-cite | Why it matters.
Not everything that looks like a conflict is one, and a false discrepancy in this table destroys the reader's trust in the true ones. The same amount restated in another currency, or in lakh or crore beside the numeral, or in words beside the figures, is a restatement and not a discrepancy; report it only where the two do not reconcile on the document's own conversion rate and reference date, or where the document supplies no rate or date at all for a conversion that governs a payment.

NOT IN THE RECORD: list the material terms you looked for and did not find, each with the consequence of its absence in one line. Where a term may sit in a page range you did not read, say that instead of saying it is absent. Do not pad this with terms the instrument would never contain.

If nothing has been attached and no text pasted, say so in a single line and set out the checklist of what will be extracted once the document is supplied — do not invent a specimen document, and do not produce example figures. If the material supplied is only an extract of a longer instrument, say which parts you could not see.

Render each section as an <h2> followed by a <table> with a header row, values in <tbody> rows. Keep the value column and the pin-cite column as separate columns in every table, so a figure can never appear without its source beside it. If the length instruction requires brevity, keep every category and cut WITHIN each one by materiality — the highest-value rows only, and a count of what was left out under that table ("9 further payment-milestone rows omitted") — because dropping a whole category can cost the reader the one number that decides the matter. Never drop the pin-cite column and never truncate or abbreviate a figure. Every row must be verifiable in under a minute by turning to the page you cite.
