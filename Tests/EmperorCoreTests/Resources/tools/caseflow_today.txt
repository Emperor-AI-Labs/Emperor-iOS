TRACK ANCHORS — a recognition checklist for reaching the right code, not a substitute for reading the record. Where the applicable version of a statute turns on the date of institution or the date of the offence, say which version you have applied and why.
- Ordinary civil suit — CPC, 1908: Order V summons, Order VIII Rule 1 written statement (30 days, extendable to 90), Order X, Order XI, Order XIV issues, Order XVIII evidence, Order XX judgment, Section 96 and Order XLI first appeal, Order XXI execution.
- Commercial suit (specified value of Rs 3 lakh or more) — Commercial Courts Act, 2015: Section 12A pre-institution mediation, mandatory unless urgent interim relief is sought; written statement within 120 days of service with forfeiture of the right thereafter (proviso to Order VIII Rule 1 as amended); Order XI disclosure on affidavit; Order XV-A case management hearing; written arguments; judgment within 90 days of conclusion of arguments; appeal to the Commercial Appellate Division within 60 days under Section 13.
- Writ — Articles 226 and 32 with the High Court's own rules: no fixed limitation but delay and laches, notice or rule, counter-affidavit and rejoinder on the Court's directions, intra-court appeal where maintainable, and an SLP under Article 136 within 90 days in a civil matter and 60 days in a criminal one (Order XXI Rule 3, Supreme Court Rules, 2013), or 60 days from the refusal of a certificate of fitness. Article 226 is invoked as often on the criminal side as the civil, so identify which before stating the SLP period.
- Insolvency before the NCLT — IBC, 2016: the default threshold of Rs 1 crore under Section 4, which decides whether an application under Section 7 or 9 is maintainable at all; Section 8 demand notice with 10 days to reply; application under Section 7 / 9 / 10; admission or rejection within 14 days under Section 7(4) / 9(5), which is directory and not mandatory (Surendra Trading), so do not report a Bench as being in default against it; moratorium under Section 14; public announcement and constitution of the CoC; CIRP of 180 days extendable by 90 under Section 12 with a 330-day outer limit; liquidation under Section 33; appeal to the NCLAT within 30 days plus 15 condonable under Section 61; appeal to the Hon'ble Supreme Court within 45 days plus 15 condonable under Section 62; NCLT Rules, 2016 throughout. Limitation on the application itself runs under Article 137 of the Limitation Act, 1963, subject to acknowledgment under Section 18.
- Company petition — Companies Act, 2013 (Sections 241-242 oppression and mismanagement, schemes, and the rest) with the NCLT Rules, 2016. Do not conflate this track with the IBC one.
- Arbitration — A&C Act, 1996: Section 21 invocation, Section 11 appointment, Section 23 statements of claim and defence, then the tribunal's own procedural orders and timetable, which govern; award within 12 months of completion of pleadings under Section 29A, extendable by 6 months by consent and thereafter only by the Court (an endeavour rather than a mandate for international commercial arbitration); correction under Section 33 within 30 days; challenge under Section 34(3) within 3 months plus 30 days condonable and not thereafter; enforcement under Section 36; appeal under Section 37.
- Criminal — WHICH CODE APPLIES IS TWO SEPARATE QUESTIONS, and mixing them is the classic error on a live file:
  (a) the OFFENCE and its punishment come from the law in force on the DATE OF THE OFFENCE — the IPC for offences before 01.07.2024, the BNS for offences on or after it, Article 20(1) of the Constitution forbidding retrospective substantive criminal law;
  (b) the PROCEDURE comes from the date the proceeding was INITIATED — Section 531(2)(a) BNSS saves only an appeal, application, trial, inquiry or investigation PENDING immediately before 01.07.2024, which continues under the CrPC; anything initiated on or after that date runs under the BNSS whenever the offence was committed. So the ordinary file — offence in March 2024, FIR registered in September 2024 — is IPC offences tried under BNSS procedure, and the charge-sheet clock, the default-bail provision and every application take their numbers from the BNSS. Evidence follows procedure, the BSA governing proceedings initiated on or after that date; where anything material turns on the changeover, flag it rather than assuming it.
  State in Section 1 which code you have applied to which question and the date that drove it. Give the BNSS provision with the CrPC equivalent in brackets; the renumbering is extensive, so where you are not certain of the corresponding number give the one you are sure of and mark the other "[to be verified]".
  Identify the KIND of criminal case before listing any stages, because the maps differ: a police case (FIR under Section 173 BNSS / 154 CrPC) runs investigation, arrest and remand, the 60 / 90-day charge-sheet clock under Section 187(3) BNSS (167(2) CrPC) with the indefeasible right to default bail on its expiry, report on completion of investigation under Section 193 BNSS (173 CrPC), cognizance, supply of documents, committal where the offence is exclusively triable by the Court of Session, discharge or framing of charge, prosecution evidence, examination of the accused, defence evidence, arguments, judgment. A private complaint runs differently — examination of the complainant on oath under Section 223 BNSS (200 CrPC), with the BNSS proviso that no cognizance may be taken without giving the proposed accused an opportunity of being heard, a step with no CrPC analogue and one regularly missed; inquiry under Section 225 BNSS (202 CrPC) where the accused resides outside jurisdiction; then issue of process or dismissal. Summons case, warrant case, sessions trial and summary trial each carry their own sequence and their own discharge provision (including the BNSS windows of 60 days from commitment, and 60 days from the supply of documents in a warrant case on a police report) — name which applies and why.
  Where the party is an accused, bail is normally the most time-critical item and must appear in Sections 4 and 5: anticipatory bail under Section 482 BNSS (438 CrPC), regular bail under Sections 480 and 483 BNSS (437 and 439 CrPC), default bail on the expiry of the charge-sheet period, release of an undertrial under Section 479 BNSS (436A CrPC), the special bail bars of any special Act in play (Section 37 NDPS, Section 45 PMLA, Section 43D(5) UAPA), cancellation, and compliance with every condition already imposed.
  Limitation for taking cognizance: Chapter XXXVIII BNSS (Sections 467-473 CrPC) — six months, one year or three years by reference to the punishment, with no bar for the graver offences. Post-trial: appeal against conviction within 30 days to the Court of Session and 60 days to the High Court, and against acquittal within 90 days (Articles 115 and 114, Limitation Act, 1963), revision, quashing under Section 528 BNSS (482 CrPC), and a criminal SLP within 60 days.
- Cheque dishonour — Section 138 NI Act: notice within 30 days of the return memo, 15 days to pay, complaint within one month of the expiry of that period under Section 142, interim compensation under Sections 143A and 148.
- Consumer — Consumer Protection Act, 2019. Fix the forum first, because that is the determination this track exists to make: pecuniary jurisdiction goes by the value of the goods or services PAID as consideration and not by the compensation claimed (Section 34(1)) — District Commission up to Rs 50 lakh, State Commission above Rs 50 lakh and up to Rs 2 crore, National Commission above Rs 2 crore; territorial jurisdiction under Section 34(2), including the complainant's own residence or place of work. - Secured-debt enforcement — SARFAESI, 2002: Section 13(2) notice with 60 days, reply under Section 13(3A), measures under Section 13(4), application to the DRT within 45 days under Section 17, appeal to the DRAT within 30 days under Section 18 with the pre-deposit; original applications under the RDB Act before the DRT.
- Motor accident claims — Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 before the Claims Tribunal: application under Section 166, and the six-month bar in Section 166(3) reintroduced by the 2019 amendment and in force from 01.04.2022, which is a genuine trap because such claims previously had no limitation — check the date of the accident against that commencement before saying anything about limitation; the detailed accident report and the insurer's offer under Sections 159 and 149; no-fault compensation under Section 164; appeal to the High Court within 90 days under Section 173, with the deposit condition in its proviso.
- Real estate — RERA, 2016: complaint to the Authority under Section 31, adjudicating officer for compensation under Section 71, relief under Section 18, appeal to the Appellate Tribunal within 60 days under Section 44 with the promoter's pre-deposit under the proviso to Section 43(5), and appeal to the High Court within 60 days under Section 58.
- Rent and eviction — the State Rent Act before the Rent Controller or Small Causes Court, with its own leave-to-defend window in a summary eviction petition; where the premises fall outside the Rent Act, a civil suit preceded by a notice to quit under Section 106 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882.
