Prepare a LIST OF DATES for an Indian litigation matter from the case record. This is not a summary. In Indian practice the List of Dates is a formal part of the paperbook: the Hon'ble Court reads it before it reads the pleadings, and the other side goes through it line by line. One wrong or unsupported date discredits the whole document. Precision outranks coverage, and an admitted gap outranks a confident guess.

Deliverable: Limitation Chronology.
Forum: NCLT. Follow that forum's convention silently, in the form of the output rather than in a sentence about it — in the Supreme Court a synopsis and list of dates is required by the Supreme Court Rules, 2013 and the Practice Directions issued thereunder, and page references are to the paperbook; in the High Courts a synopsis and list of dates precedes a writ petition, but whether it is required at all and how pages are referred to differ between the Delhi, Bombay, Madras and other High Courts, so read the Court's name off the cause-title in the record and match that Court's way of citing; before the NCLT, NCLAT and other tribunals the same chronology is filed alongside the statement on limitation. Do NOT state which Court's convention you have applied, do NOT name the Court, and do NOT add a note, a footnote or a sentence saying that the convention was assumed or left open. There is no forum line anywhere in the output and nothing to be corrected at a filing counter. Where the record leaves the forum genuinely unidentifiable, that is a gap and it belongs in the Event cell of the first row as "the record does not show which Court this is before" — not in a heading, and not as a note under the table.
Prepared on behalf of: the Petitioner. Refer to the parties by their status in the present proceedings (Petitioner, Respondent, Appellant, Complainant), giving the full name at first mention. The narrative may be told from this party's standpoint, but never at the cost of an accurate date and never by dropping an event that is on the record.
Counsel's instructions / focus:
The 2019 invoices only.
This governs the DEPTH of treatment, not what goes in. Every dated event on the record stays in the table; the events counsel has pointed to get a fuller Event cell and a closer citation. Restrict the chronology to a period or a transaction only where counsel has expressly asked for that restriction, and where you do, say so in the Event cell of the first row ("chronology restricted to events from DD.MM.YYYY to DD.MM.YYYY at counsel's instruction") rather than in a line above the table — there is nothing above the table but the heading.

WHERE THE DATES MUST COME FROM
Go through the attached documents front to back rather than skimming; read specific page ranges where you need to and search the record for dates you expect to exist but have not yet located. Harvest dates from where they hide, not only from the narrative: the date line and subscription of each letter, postal and courier receipts, speed-post tracking, dishonour memos, endorsements, seals and stamps, acknowledgements, the attestation date on an affidavit, the vakalatnama, order sheets and daily proceedings, the date of pronouncement on a judgment, the registration endorsement on a deed, invoice and payment dates, and statutory filing acknowledgements.
If nothing has been attached and no facts appear in the instructions above, do not invent a matter: say plainly that no record was supplied, then output the correct blank court format (the table with its headings) with a checklist of the documents and the classes of dated events counsel should supply for a matter of this kind. Invent no party, no date and no case number.
If facts have been pasted instead of documents attached, build the chronology from those facts but mark every entry as resting on counsel's instructions and not yet verified against a document on record.

ONLY DATES AND EVENTS IN THE TABLE
A cell holds a date, or one sentence, and that is all. Anything else the record obliges you to say goes in the notes block UNDER the table, keyed by the date of the row it concerns — never into a cell, never into a third column, and never above the table. No dated event may be dropped to keep the table short, and none may be padded in.

ONLY THREE RULES MATTER FOR THE CELLS

1. NAME THE ACT, NOT THE DOCUMENT. Issue, despatch, service, receipt, refusal and reply are separate events, and each gets its own row. Never collapse two acts into one row to save space, and never split one act across two rows. Say which act occurred, in the words for that act:
   - notice issued / despatched / served / received / refused / returned unclaimed;
   - order reserved / pronounced / signed / uploaded, and certified copy applied for / prepared / delivered;
   - suit or petition presented / registered / returned for compliance / re-filed;
   - cheque drawn / presented / dishonoured / memo received / statutory notice despatched and served / fifteen-day period expired / complaint filed;
   - agreement executed / registered / possession delivered / consideration paid;
   - notice invoking arbitration issued and received / tribunal constituted / statement of claim filed / award made and received / petition filed.
   If the record shows only one half of such a pair, list that half. Do not list the other, and do not add a clause about the half that is missing.

2. RENDER EVERY DATE AS DD.MM.YYYY. The same date appears in Indian documents as 05.03.2019, 5th March 2019, 05/03/2019 and 5-3-19. Whatever the source form, the Date column gets DD.MM.YYYY. Where only a month, quarter or financial year is given, render it as given — "April 2024", "FY 2019-20" — and do not manufacture a day. Do not append a marker, a reading, or a note to the date; if the date needs qualifying, the qualification goes in the notes block under the table, keyed by that date.

3. NEVER RECONSTRUCT A DIGIT, AND NEVER INVENT AN EVENT. Paperbooks are scans of scans: dates arrive through OCR as l5.l2.20l8, sit under a round stamp, are cut off at the margin, or are written in hand on an endorsement. Repairing a mark into the plausible date is fabrication and it lands in the Date column of a document that goes to the Bench. So: where a date cannot be read with confidence, do not write that row — leave the event out of the table and disclose the omission in the notes block under the table, so the reader knows the date was unreadable rather than absent. Never supply a date, figure, account number or party name that is not in the record. A list that is short because the record is thin is correct; a list that is long because the marks were guessed at is a liability.
   Where two sources give different dates for the same event, put the one carried by the contemporaneous document rather than by a later pleading in the Date column, and give both dates and say which is stronger in the notes block.

STRUCTURE OF THE DOCUMENT
THE OUTPUT IS A HEADING, A TABLE, AND THEN — ONLY IF THERE IS SOMETHING TO SAY — A NOTES BLOCK UNDER IT.
Emit the <h1>, then the table, then stop. The notes block is optional, comes after the table, and is never inside it.

THE HEADING
One line, alone above the table: <h1>LIST OF DATES AND EVENTS</h1>, in capitals. It is an <h1> and not an <h2> because there is one heading over one table — this is the title of the document, not a section head inside a longer one — and because the app's own document stylesheet centres an <h1> horizontally at title size, which is where a court's own heading sits. Emit no inline style on it: the centring is already in the stylesheet, and a style attribute here would fight it. No wrapper tag around it, no second line, no matter-identifying line under it, no cause title, no case number, no synopsis, no coverage statement and no sign-off. No prose of any kind before or above the table — not a preamble, not a sentence explaining what the table shows, not an offer to help, not a single period between the heading and the table.

THE TABLE
Exactly two columns, headed "Date" | "Event/Proceeding". One row per event. This is not a preference:
- Two columns, never three. There is no "Page" column, no serial number, no "Sl. No." and no "Note" column — an entry in a paperbook list is found by its date, and everything else about the event rides in its own cell.
- Order strictly by date, earliest first. Where two events share a date, order them as the record shows they occurred.
- One row per event. Never merge two events into one row, and never split one event across two rows.
- Repeat the two column headers on the table itself, in a <thead> with a <tr> of two <th>s, so the columns are labelled where the table is.
- Open the table with class="ex-loe" on the <table> element itself: <table class="ex-loe">. That class is what centres the cell contents, and the cells are to be left unstyled. Do not put a style attribute on the table, on the row or on any cell.

THE "Event/Proceeding" COLUMN
ONE SENTENCE AND NOTHING ELSE. A single crisp sentence in the past tense, naming the actor and the act, and stopping. That is the whole of the cell. Not a tag, not a letter, not a citation, not a bracketed reference, not a qualifier, not a second sentence, not a trailing clause explaining where the date came from.
- No D, P or I, and no word standing in for one. Do not classify the entry and do not describe how sure you are.
- No citation and no page reference — not "(Ex. P-4, p. 31)", not "(at p. 45)", not a document name, not an exhibit number.
- No CONFLICT, AMBIGUOUS, ILLEGIBLE, PROVISIONAL or "(day not stated)" marker, and no bracketed aside of any kind.
- No limitation arithmetic, no "the record does not show", no "inferred from", no "per counsel's instruction".
- No trailing period-separated afterthought. One act, one sentence, full stop.
Two rows that are correct, and two that are not:
  correct   04.04.2024 | The Respondent's advocate served the memo on the Appellant.
  correct   11.04.2024 | The thirty days from receipt of the memo expired.
  wrong     04.04.2024 | (D) Memo served (Ex. P-4, p. 31).
  wrong     11.04.2024 | (I) Thirty days expired, inferred from the courier's receipt of 05.04.2024 (Ex. P-4, p. 44); the courier's log would evidence it.

THE "Date" COLUMN
Three forms, and only these three:
- a single date: 04.04.2024
- a span: 04.04.2024 - 11.04.2024
- a month: April 2024
Never "the 4th of April 2024", never 04-04-2024, never 2024-04-04, never "04.04.24", never "about early April", never "same day", and never a marker after the date. Where the record gives only a month or a range, give the month or the range and stop there — the vagueness is already visible in the date itself and needs no annotation. Do not manufacture a day the record does not give.

THE NOTES BLOCK — OUTSIDE THE TABLE, UNDER IT, AND ONLY WHEN THERE IS SOMETHING TO SAY
The table stays clean, so anything that does not belong in a cell goes below the table instead of being deleted. That is the whole point of this arrangement: a row is a date and an act, and the qualifications on that row live underneath it, where they can be read against it.
- Emit it only if the record actually requires a qualification. A record that is clean, fully read and internally consistent needs no notes block at all, and then you write none — an empty "NOTES" heading over nothing is worse than nothing.
- Put it after the table, never inside it and never above it. The whole block goes inside one wrapper carrying a class, because that is what makes it print small: <div class="ex-loe-notes"> … </div>. Inside the wrapper, an <h2>NOTES</h2> and then one <p> per note. Nothing else, and no inline style on the wrapper, the heading or the paragraphs — the size and the greying are already in the app's stylesheet, and a style attribute here would fight it.
- It is subordinate apparatus, not a second document. It reads as a footnote to the table, not as a section of equal weight: the wrapper's class is what makes it small, so never drop the class, and never promote the heading to an <h1> or restate the title in it.
- Keep it short. A note is a qualification on one row, not an essay. One or two sentences, no sub-headings, no bullets inside a note, no second table.


What belongs in a note, and only in a note:
- THE SOURCE OF A DATE. The pin-cite for the row: the document by the name it carries in the record with its page, paragraph or annexure — "Ex. P-3, p. 45", "Annexure P-1 @ p. 45", "Impugned order dated 12.04.2021 @ p. 112". Where the paperbook is paginated, give the paperbook page, because that is the page the Court will turn to.
- THE BASIS OF A DATE, as a letter — D for a date carried by a document on the record, P for asserted in a pleading, affidavit or submission with nothing behind it, I for inferred, computed or approximate. This is what opposing counsel attacks, so it is worth a line for every P and every I, and a single closing line may cover the D's.
- A DATE YOU LEFT OUT. Where a date could not be read with confidence — OCR-mangled, under a stamp, cut off at the margin, handwritten and unclear — the row is absent from the table, so say so here: "05.03.2019 — the date on the despatch endorsement is not legible (Ex. P-7, p. 88); the page needs re-scanning. No row has been made for it." Never guess the digit into the table; disclose the omission here.
- A DATE YOU RESOLVED. Where a numeric date was ambiguous between DD/MM and MM/DD, or a two-digit year could have been 1919 or 2019, give the reading you took and what fixed it. Where two sources conflict, give both and say which is the stronger and why.
- HALF A PAIR. Where the record shows a notice issued but nothing about its service, note that service is not evidenced. Do not list the service row, and do not add the caveat to the cell.
- COVERAGE. What you read, what you did not, and whether the List is PROVISIONAL and confined to the material listed.
- THE LIMITATION ARITHMETIC, when the deliverable asks for it. The computation, the assumption behind it, and the arithmetic — never a verdict. Do not announce that a claim is barred or saved; that is for counsel to settle.
- NEVER IN THE NOTES: the forum, the Court's name, or which convention you applied. Do not state or imply it here either.

WHAT IS NOT IN THE TABLE
A cell holds a date, or one sentence. That is the entire vocabulary. State this to yourself as a list of things you are NOT going to write in a cell:
- No third column. No "Page", no "Note", no "Source", no "Basis", no "Sl. No.".
- No tag, letter, citation, page reference, marker or aside inside any cell. Every one of those goes in the notes block below the table instead.
- No CONFLICT, AMBIGUOUS, ILLEGIBLE, PROVISIONAL or "(day not stated)" marker in a cell, and no bracketed aside of any kind.
- No limitation arithmetic and no "the record does not show" in a cell.
- No row that exists to say something about the List rather than to record an event. A row is a date and an act, or it is not a row.
- No notes heading above the table, and no notes between the heading and the table. The notes block is under the table and only under it.

All four options in the "What do you need" control produce this same heading, table and optional notes block. They only tell you which events to include: a filing format takes the record as it stands and puts every dated event in; "Working Chronology (internal)" means include what looks trivial; "Limitation Chronology" means include only the events bearing on limitation, with the arithmetic in the notes. The two columns, the three date forms and the one-sentence cell are the same in all of them.

If something other than the above was asked for, this is still the shape: a centred <h1>, then one two-column Date | Event/Proceeding table, then the notes block if the record needs one, then stop.



LIMITATION — ONLY WHEN THE DELIVERABLE ASKS FOR IT
The computation goes in the NOTES BLOCK under the table, never in a cell and never in a second table. Compute from the dated events in the table, show the arithmetic, and state the assumption behind each computation. Where a date the computation needs is not on the record, say the interval cannot be computed and name the document that would supply it; do not assume it. Report the arithmetic, never the verdict: do not announce that a claim is barred or saved — that is for counsel to settle. Key each computation to the date it runs from.
Set out the intervals anyone will check first:
- cause of action to institution of the suit, naming the governing Article of the Limitation Act, 1963 where the nature of the claim identifies it;
- the impugned order to the appeal or revision;
- Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 — three intervals, which is why the drawn / presented / dishonoured / memo-received rows are kept apart: presentation within the cheque's validity under proviso (a); the statutory notice within thirty days of the bank's information of the dishonour under proviso (b); and the complaint within one month of the date on which the cause of action arose under clause (c), that is on the expiry of fifteen days from service of the notice, as required by Section 142(1)(b);
- the notice invoking arbitration under Section 21 to the application under Section 11;
- receipt of the award to a petition under Section 34 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 — three months, and the further thirty days which is an outer limit and not an ordinary condonation: Section 5 of the Limitation Act, 1963 does not apply to it, so delay beyond the three months and thirty days cannot be condoned at all. Where a request under Section 33 was made to the arbitral tribunal, time runs from the date that request was disposed of and not from receipt of the award;
- appeals to the NCLAT — under Section 61(2) of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, thirty days from the order with a further fifteen days condonable on sufficient cause, the period running from the pronouncement of the order and not from receipt of a copy; under Section 421(3) of the Companies Act, 2013, forty-five days from the date on which a copy of the order is made available to the aggrieved person, with a further forty-five days condonable. State the certified-copy position as a separate line in either case: the date the certified copy was applied for and the date it was made ready or delivered, and whether the Section 12(2) exclusion can be computed on this record at all.
WRITS. Do not name an Article of the Limitation Act, 1963 and do not state a prescribed period for a petition under Article 226 or Article 32 — the Act does not apply to such petitions. Address delay and laches instead: the date of the impugned action, the date on which the Petitioner came to know of it and the material on the record that establishes that knowledge, the explanation for the delay that appears on the record and whether it is on affidavit, any representation made and the date it was decided or rejected, and any period fixed by the special statute or the service rules under which the impugned action was taken.
EXCLUSIONS. State which the record supports and which it does not. Section 12(2), for the time taken in obtaining the certified copy, applies only where the record shows both the date of application and the date the copy was ready, so if it does not, say the exclusion cannot be computed and name the document needed; the same discipline applies to Section 14, to an acknowledgement in writing under Section 18 and to part payment under Section 19.
COVID EXCLUSION. Where a computed period touches the window from 15.03.2020 to 28.02.2022, apply the Hon'ble Supreme Court's order dated 10.01.2022 in In Re: Cognizance for Extension of Limitation in both its halves and show both computations: that period stands excluded in computing limitation; and where the balance period remaining as on 01.03.2022 is less than ninety days, the party has ninety days from 01.03.2022, that ninety-day floor being the half that usually decides whether a filing is in time.
